# Choose the Right AI Model for Your Use Case

## Introduction: The Garden Metaphor

You can think of an AI model as a vegetable you want to grow in your garden. Before you buy the seeds, you need to research the weather and water requirements for that plant or else it might die before it ever flowers. As it grows, you need to evaluate and re-optimize the care you're providing so that it can thrive and bloom.

For an entire garden, you need to do that with every vegetable and make sure that none of them interact with each other harmfully. This is important because you need all of those different vegetables in order to survive—you can't live on carrots alone.

## Multi-Model Approach

If you want your AI garden to grow, you need to ensure that you're using a variety of models. This is what we call a **multi-model approach**, where you have a variety of models for your AI use cases.

This means you can pick and choose from different models to find the right one for the right use case. This gives you the opportunity to look at how each of those models is designed to find the right fit.

### Key Questions to Ask

When evaluating models, you need to ask specific, important questions:
- Who built it?
- What data was it trained on?
- What guardrails are in place for it?
- What risks and regulations do you need to consider and account for?

## The Model Selection Process

### Step 1: Write a Specific Prompt

The first step in choosing a model for your use case is identifying the best use case to fit your business needs. This begins with a **prompt**.

A prompt is a textual input or instruction that goes into a large language model to set up the basics of the AI. A good prompt:
- Clearly articulates your use case
- Defines the problem you're solving with AI
- Captures the user problem and ask of the technology
- Establishes guardrails for what good looks like

### Step 2: Research Available Models

Next, research the available models, looking at things like:
- Model size
- Performance
- Costs
- Risks
- Deployment methods

### Step 3: Evaluate and Test Models

You can then use the information you've collected to evaluate those models against your prompt and identify which ones you want to test.

**Testing Strategy:**
1. Start with a large model and work with it until you satisfy your original prompt
2. Then, try to duplicate the result using smaller models
3. You're essentially passing the same prompt through different models to experiment and see which works best

This enables you to choose the best model for the use case.

## Key Factors in Model Selection

Throughout the process, consider factors that affect your choice of model:

**Primary Elements:**
- Performance
- Accuracy
- Reliability
- Speed

**Additional Considerations:**
- Size
- Deployment method
- Transparency
- Potential risks

All of these need to be considered as you choose the right model for your use case and begin implementation.

## Continuous Monitoring and Governance

Model selection is not a one-time decision. You need to continually evaluate and govern that model with ongoing testing to assess how it's working based on performance and cost benchmarks.

This requires:
- Continuously updating the data and prompt where needed to keep it relevant
- Testing new models as they become available
- Avoiding being locked into just one model as situations change both inside and outside your business

### The Importance of Ongoing Care

Remember: even once your AI model crop is growing happily, you need to keep taking care of it. This means continuous testing, governance, and optimization—all of which are essential to keep that model up to date and running optimally.

## Implementation: Building the Right Team

Model implementation requires a team that not only crosses disciplines but also crosses lines of business. Don't think of it as proprietary to any one department—treat it as a distinctly collaborative project that takes multiple teams to get up and running.

### Team Responsibilities

Ensure that this team is ready and able to:
- Diagnose performance benchmarks
- Understand that each benchmark measures something unique
- Produce datasets that show how everything is being calculated
- Make informed decisions about future models and use cases

## Final Thoughts

Models evolve, so your strategy and choices need to do the same. You want to keep growing towards the sun instead of withering on the vine.
